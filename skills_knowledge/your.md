## OVERVIEW

"Your" is a second-person singular and plural possessive determiner in English. It modifies nouns by indicating ownership, association, or relation belonging to the person or people being addressed. It is one of the most frequently used words in English and one of the most frequently misused, primarily through confusion with "you're." Mastery of "your" spans grammar, style, tone, rhetoric, and professional communication.

---

## CORE CONCEPTS

**Grammatical classification**
"Your" is a possessive determiner, not a pronoun. It always precedes and modifies a noun or noun phrase. It does not stand alone as a subject or object in a sentence.

Correct: Your idea is excellent.
Incorrect as standalone: That idea is your. (Here "yours" is required — the possessive pronoun form.)

**The possessive determiner vs. possessive pronoun distinction**
- Your (determiner): precedes a noun. "Your coat is here."
- Yours (pronoun): replaces a noun. "That coat is yours."

These are not interchangeable.

**Number neutrality**
"Your" serves both singular and plural addressees with identical form. Context determines whether one person or many are addressed.

"Your assignment is due Friday." (one student)
"Your assignments are due Friday." (whole class)

**The your/you're problem**
This is the single most common error involving this word.
- Your = possessive determiner
- You're = contraction of "you are"

Test: substitute "you are" in the sentence. If it reads correctly, use "you're." If it breaks the sentence, use "your."

"Your welcome" — wrong. "You are welcome" — correct, therefore: "You're welcome."
"You're bag is here" — wrong. "You are bag is here" makes no sense, therefore: "Your bag is here."

**Formal register note**
In very formal writing, "your" can carry a deferential or honorific weight: "Your Honour," "Your Majesty," "Your Excellency." These are fixed phrases with specific protocols.

## STEP-BY-STEP WORKFLOW

**Step 1 — Identify the noun being modified**
Before writing "your," confirm there is a noun or noun phrase immediately following it (or close after an adjective). "Your brilliant suggestion" — noun phrase present, usage valid.

**Step 2 — Apply the "you are" substitution test**
Replace "your" mentally with "you are." If the sentence becomes nonsensical or grammatically broken, "your" is correct. If it becomes a coherent sentence, you need "you're."
