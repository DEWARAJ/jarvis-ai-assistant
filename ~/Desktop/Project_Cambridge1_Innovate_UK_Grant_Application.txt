SECTION 1 — THE PROBLEM AND MARKET OPPORTUNITY
-----------------------------------------------
Air quality in urban environments remains one of the most pressing public health challenges in the United Kingdom. Road transport accounts for approximately 80% of roadside nitrogen dioxide (NOx) emissions and a significant proportion of fine particulate matter (PM2.5), both of which are directly linked to cardiovascular and respiratory disease. The UK Government's commitment to Net Zero by 2050 and the forthcoming Euro 7 emissions legislation — the most stringent ever introduced — means that both existing internal combustion engine vehicles and hybrid fleets face an urgent technology gap.

The global exhaust aftertreatment market is valued at approximately $42 billion (2024) and is forecast to reach $67 billion by 2030 (CAGR ~8%). Despite this scale, no commercially viable membrane-based exhaust capture solution exists for point-of-emission NOx and particulate filtration at the tailpipe level. Current solutions — catalytic converters, diesel particulate filters, selective catalytic reduction systems — are bulky, thermally dependent, expensive, and increasingly inadequate against Euro 7 thresholds.

Project Cambridge-1 addresses this gap directly.

SECTION 2 — THE SOLUTION AND TECHNICAL NOVELTY
-----------------------------------------------
Project Cambridge-1 is developing a six-layer composite exhaust membrane unit designed for direct tailpipe integration. The membrane operates at exhaust temperatures between 150°C and 650°C, captures particulate matter at the PM1.0 level and above, and achieves NOx adsorption through a proprietary multi-layer ionic exchange architecture.

Key technical differentiators:

1. Thermal stability across the full exhaust operating range without ceramic substrate dependency.
2. Passive regeneration cycle — no external energy input required for filter clearance.
3. Modular unit design compatible with existing exhaust architectures, requiring no engine management system modification.
4. Projected capture efficiency of 94% PM2.5, 87% NOx at standard urban drive cycle conditions (to be validated in Phase A testing).

The innovation is protected through a combination of trade secret (manufacturing process) and a patent application currently in preparation covering the multi-layer ionic exchange architecture. The technology has no direct commercial equivalent in the current UK or European market.

Academic validation and materials characterisation will be conducted in partnership with the University of Cambridge, whose engineering and materials science departments bring world-class expertise in membrane science and combustion chemistry.

SECTION 4 — PROJECT PLAN AND MILESTONES
----------------------------------------
Project Duration: 18 months

Phase A — Months 1 to 6: Materials Selection and Prototype Fabrication
- Finalise six-layer membrane architecture specification
- Source and characterise candidate materials with Cambridge partner
- Fabricate first prototype units (x3) in controlled lab environment
- Milestone: Prototype units survive 200-hour thermal cycling test

Phase B — Months 7 to 12: Performance Testing and Optimisation
- Install prototype units on test vehicle exhaust rigs
- Conduct controlled emissions testing against Euro 6d and Euro 7 draft thresholds
- Iterate membrane composition based on captured data
- Milestone: Achieve 90%+ PM2.5 capture and 85%+ NOx reduction in controlled test conditions

Phase C — Months 13 to 18: Real-World Validation and Commercial Data Package
- Extended real-world drive cycle testing across urban, suburban and motorway profiles
- Independent third-party emissions verification
- Produce full technical data package suitable for Euro 7 certification discussions
- Milestone: Data package delivered; first licensing conversation initiated with named Tier 1 supplier

SECTION 6 — ROUTE TO MARKET AND COMMERCIAL IMPACT
--------------------------------------------------
Project Cambridge-1 is designed from the outset as a licensable technology, not a manufactured product. The commercial model is B2B technology licensing to Tier 1 and Tier 2 automotive exhaust system suppliers — companies such as Tenneco, Faurecia, Eberspächer, and their UK supply chain partners — who embed the membrane unit into their existing product lines.

This model means the applicant does not require manufacturing scale-up investment. Revenue is generated through an upfront licence fee plus a per-unit royalty on every membrane unit produced under licence.

Conservative projections based on a single mid-tier licensing deal:
- Licence fee:         £500,000 upfront
- Royalty rate:        £4.50 per unit
- Target volume Year 3: 200,000 units per annum
- Year 3 royalty revenue: £900,000 per annum

The technology directly supports the UK Government's Net Zero 2050 targets, the Clean Air Strategy, and the Automotive Transformation Fund objectives. It creates high-value engineering IP in the UK, retains value through the patent portfolio, and positions the UK as a technology exporter in a market currently dominated by German and Japanese suppliers.

Independent economic modelling suggests a successful licensing outcome could remove the equivalent of 40,000 tonnes of NOx from UK urban air annually by 2032 at full deployment scale.
