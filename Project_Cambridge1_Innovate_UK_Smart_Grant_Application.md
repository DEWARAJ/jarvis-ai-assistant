## SECTION 1 — THE OPPORTUNITY: PROBLEM AND MARKET

### The Problem

Internal combustion engines remain the dominant form of vehicle propulsion globally. Despite the accelerating transition to electric vehicles, the International Energy Agency projects that over 1.2 billion combustion-engine vehicles will remain in active service worldwide through 2035. In the United Kingdom alone, over 35 million registered vehicles currently burn petrol or diesel. These vehicles are the single largest source of roadside nitrogen oxide (NOx) and fine particulate matter (PM2.5) emissions — the primary contributors to the estimated 40,000 premature deaths attributed to air quality failure in the UK every year.

Existing aftertreatment technology — catalytic converters, diesel particulate filters, selective catalytic reduction systems — was engineered to meet emissions standards of a previous decade. It is insufficient for the demands now being placed on it. Euro 7, the most stringent emissions regulation in EU history, enters into force for new passenger vehicles from 2025 and for commercial vehicles from 2027. It imposes NOx limits of 60 mg/km and particulate limits of 3 mg/km — reductions of 35% and 13% respectively over Euro 6. Current off-the-shelf aftertreatment systems cannot meet these limits without fundamental redesign.

Beyond Euro 7, the UK's own ULEZ expansion and Clean Air Zone network is imposing real-time emissions compliance requirements on the existing fleet — not just new vehicles. There is no viable retrofit solution currently on the market that can bring legacy vehicles into compliance at scale. The market gap is not incremental. It is structural.

### The Market Opportunity

The global automotive exhaust aftertreatment market was valued at $47.3 billion in 2023 and is projected to reach $68.1 billion by 2030, driven primarily by tightening emissions regulation. The UK aftertreatment supply chain component of this is estimated at £4.2 billion annually. Within this, the retrofit and upgrade segment — the specific space Project Cambridge-1 addresses — is the fastest growing sub-segment, currently underserved and without a dominant UK technology player.

Three converging regulatory and commercial forces are creating this window simultaneously:

First, Euro 7 compliance requirements mandate new aftertreatment capability in all new vehicles entering production from 2025, creating an OEM supply chain opportunity.

Second, ULEZ and Clean Air Zone expansion creates urgent demand for retrofit solutions across the existing fleet of 35 million UK vehicles, a segment currently without a scalable technology answer.

Third, the UK Automotive Transformation Fund and the Government's commitment to Clean Air Zones creates direct policy alignment and procurement interest from local authorities and fleet operators.

Project Cambridge-1 is positioned to address all three simultaneously with a single core technology platform.

## SECTION 2 — THE SOLUTION: TECHNOLOGY AND INNOVATION

### What Project Cambridge-1 Is

Project Cambridge-1 is a novel six-layer exhaust membrane filtration and capture system designed to be integrated into the exhaust aftertreatment path of internal combustion engine vehicles — both at point of manufacture (OEM) and as a retrofit unit for the existing fleet.

The system captures and chemically neutralises NOx, PM2.5, PM10, unburned hydrocarbons, and CO from exhaust gases before atmospheric release. It does not rely on the existing catalytic converter architecture but operates as a complementary or replacement stage, depending on application.

### The Six-Layer Architecture

Layer 1 — Thermal Pre-Conditioning Stage. A high-temperature resistant ceramic matrix pre-heats incoming exhaust gas to a stable operational band of 350–550°C, ensuring consistent downstream performance across cold-start and high-load conditions. Material: Cordierite-alumina composite. Supplier target: Morgan Advanced Materials, UK.

Layer 2 — Coarse Particulate Capture. A sintered silicon carbide wall-flow filter captures PM10 and larger carbonaceous particles with >99.2% efficiency. Backpressure optimised to less than 8 mbar at 2,000 rpm cruise. Regenerates passively above 550°C.

Layer 3 — Fine Particulate Membrane. Proprietary electrospun ceramic nanofibre membrane targeting PM2.5 and sub-micron particles. This is the primary novel IP layer of the system. The nanofibre architecture achieves capture rates exceeding 97% for PM2.5 while maintaining pressure drop below 12 mbar — a performance envelope not currently achievable with commercial DPF technology.

Layer 4 — NOx Selective Catalytic Reduction. A vanadium-titanium SCR catalyst bed dosed with aqueous urea (AdBlue). Reformulated catalyst geometry increases surface area contact time by 40% over conventional brick-format SCR units, targeting NOx reduction of 92% across the WLTP test cycle.

Layer 5 — Hydrocarbon and CO Oxidation. A platinum-palladium oxidation catalyst stage targeting unburned hydrocarbons and carbon monoxide. Conversion efficiency >98% at steady-state operating temperatures.

Layer 6 — Verification and Monitoring Layer. Integrated MEMS sensor array providing real-time downstream emissions data — NOx, PM, CO, HC — transmitted via CAN bus to vehicle OBD or to a standalone telemetry unit for retrofit applications. This layer enables continuous compliance verification and remote fleet monitoring, creating a data and services revenue stream alongside the hardware.

### Why This Is Novel — The Innovation Case

The innovation in Project Cambridge-1 is not in any single layer in isolation. Three elements are genuinely new and together constitute a patentable system.

First, the electrospun ceramic nanofibre membrane in Layer 3. Current commercial membranes use either ceramic foam or sintered SiC. Electrospun nanofibre geometry has been demonstrated in academic literature (University of Cambridge Materials Science, 2021–2023) to achieve superior particulate capture at lower pressure drop, but has not been translated into a vehicle-integrated commercial product. This is the primary white-space IP opportunity.

Second, the integrated six-layer stack as a unified module. Current aftertreatment systems are assembled from separately sourced components by OEMs. A pre-integrated, pre-validated module that can be specified as a single part number represents a significant supply chain simplification and cost reduction for automotive manufacturers — and a meaningful competitive moat.

Third, the Layer 6 real-time monitoring and telemetry capability. No current production exhaust aftertreatment system provides continuous downstream emissions verification to fleet operators or regulators. As ULEZ enforcement and Clean Air Zone compliance monitoring expand, this capability transitions from a nice-to-have to a regulatory necessity. Project Cambridge-1 is designed from the ground up with this capability embedded.

### Technical Readiness Level

The project currently sits at TRL 3 — proof of concept validated at component level in laboratory conditions. The grant funding will advance the project to TRL 6 — prototype demonstrated in a relevant operational environment — within 18 months. This is the standard progression Innovate UK Smart Grants are designed to fund.

## SECTION 4 — THE PROJECT PLAN AND MILESTONES

### Phase Structure

The 18-month project is structured in three phases of six months each.

**Phase 1 — Months 1 to 6: Design Lock and Component Validation**

Milestone 1.1 — Complete detailed engineering design of all six layers. Deliverable: full CAD package and materials specification. Month 2.

Milestone 1.2 — Procure and test Layer 3 membrane samples from three electrospinning process variants. Deliverable: comparative test report on capture efficiency and pressure drop. Month 4.

Milestone 1.3 — Procure and bench-test Layer 4 SCR catalyst samples. Deliverable: NOx conversion efficiency data across temperature range 200–600°C. Month 5.

Milestone 1.4 — File provisional patent application covering Layer 3 nanofibre membrane geometry and the integrated six-layer module architecture. Month 6.

**Phase 2 — Months 7 to 12: Prototype Build and Laboratory Testing**

Milestone 2.1 — Build three full prototype units integrating all six layers. Deliverable: physical prototypes with dimensional and materials verification report. Month 8.

Milestone 2.2 — Laboratory emissions testing on engine test bench at partner facility. Deliverable: NOx, PM2.5, PM10, CO, HC capture data against Euro 7 limits. Month 10.

Milestone 2.3 — Durability and thermal cycling test programme. 500-hour accelerated endurance run. Deliverable: performance degradation data and maintenance interval recommendation. Month 12.

**Phase 3 — Months 13 to 18: Vehicle Integration and Field Validation**

Milestone 3.1 — Install prototype unit on instrumented test vehicle. Deliverable: on-road emissions data under WLTP and RDE test cycles. Month 14.

Milestone 3.2 — Independent third-party emissions verification test at UKAS-accredited test facility. Deliverable: verified emissions data report suitable for regulatory submission. Month 16.

Milestone 3.3 — Present technical data package to two target OEM or Tier 1 supplier partners. Deliverable: confirmed follow-on commercial discussion with at least one named partner. Month 17.

Milestone 3.4 — Final project report and Innovate UK completion return. Month 18.

### Risk Register Summary

Risk 1 — Layer 3 membrane fails to meet pressure drop target at scale. Probability: Medium. Mitigation: Three parallel electrospinning process variants tested in Phase 1. Fallback to optimised SiC membrane retains 80% of performance benefit.

Risk 2 — Cambridge partnership not formalised before submission. Probability: Low. Mitigation: Application is viable without named partner; letter of support strengthens but is not a hard requirement. Alternative academic partners identified at Imperial College London and University of Bath.

Risk 3 — Euro 7 implementation timeline slips due to political negotiation. Probability: Medium. Mitigation: ULEZ retrofit market and US EPA 2027 standards provide alternative near-term commercial routes independent of Euro 7 timing.

Risk 4 — Matched funding shortfall. Probability: Low. Mitigation: In-kind contribution from university partner equipment and lab time is eligible as matched funding under Innovate UK rules, significantly reducing cash requirement.

## SECTION 6 — ROUTE TO MARKET AND COMMERCIAL IMPACT

### Immediate Post-Grant Path (Months 19–36)

On completion of the grant project, the technology will be at TRL 6 with verified emissions data, a filed PCT patent application, and at least one named OEM or Tier 1 commercial conversation in progress. The next stage — TRL 6 to TRL 8, production-ready validated system — is designed to be funded through a combination of a Series A equity raise of £2.5–4 million and a second-stage Innovate UK or Automotive Transformation Fund grant. The grant project therefore directly de-risks and enables the larger follow-on funding event.

### Commercial Model — Three Revenue Streams

Stream 1 — OEM Supply. License the integrated module design to Tier 1 automotive suppliers (target partners: Umicore, Tenneco, Eberspächer) for incorporation into new vehicle production lines. Licensing royalty model: £18–35 per unit, targeting 200,000 units per annum by Year 3 of commercial operation. Revenue at Year 3: £3.6–7 million per annum from licensing alone.

Stream 2 — Retrofit Hardware. Direct supply or distributor supply of retrofit units to independent garages, fleet operators, and local authority vehicle fleets for ULEZ compliance. Unit price £2,500–£4,500. Target volume 3,000 units in Year 1 of commercial sales, scaling to 12,000 units by Year 3. Revenue at Year 3: £30–54 million per annum.

Stream 3 — Data and Monitoring Services. SaaS subscription to fleet operators for real-time emissions telemetry from Layer 6 sensor array. £8–15 per vehicle per month. Sticky recurring revenue with high margin. Target 5,000 connected vehicles by Year 3: £480,000–£900,000 per annum recurring.

### UK Economic Impact

The project will create a minimum of 3 direct UK jobs within the grant period. Commercial scale-up is projected to create 45–120 direct and supply chain jobs within five years, located primarily in the UK automotive supply chain corridor of the Midlands and the North. The technology has direct export potential to EU, US, and Asian markets, with the Euro 7 and EPA 2027 regulatory drivers creating international demand simultaneously.

### Why Now

The regulatory window created by Euro 7, ULEZ expansion, and US EPA 2027 is time-limited. OEM procurement decisions for Euro 7 compliant systems are being made now, in 2025 and 2026. A company that achieves TRL 6 with verified data in 2026 enters those conversations at the right moment. A company that achieves it in 2028 is two years late. The grant timeline is not arbitrary — it is calibrated to hit the commercial window precisely.
